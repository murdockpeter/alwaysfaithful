# Always Faithful

Standalone 2030 near-future USMC tactical game and tactical-resolution module for Sea of Uncertainty.

## Current prototype

The game now opens on a complete, persistent operational scenario across the 64×104 whole-island Taiwan board (6,656 hexes at roughly 4 km spacing), using the same Natural Earth coastline and NOAA ETOPO 2022 pipeline as Sea of Uncertainty. **Operation Island Sentinel** begins with a full operations-order briefing, commander’s intent, intelligence estimate, and order of battle. The player controls two independently selectable USMC infantry battalions; three PLA battalion-sized formations maneuver toward their objective during the opposing phase. All five formations retain position, strength, action points, and subordinate-unit identity in the operational scenario save.

The PLA’s actual positions are never shown merely because the scenario knows them. Operational fog is cleared around friendly formations, while enemy counters appear only as uncertain, identified, or observed contacts when passive detection range or an active **Recon** order earns that information. Lost tracks remain at their last-known hex for one turn and then disappear. Recon costs 2 AP, reaches 12 operational hexes, searches a two-hex radius for two turns, and leaves a violet tasking ring. The live **Order of Battle** panel reports friendly locations and strength but withholds enemy locations and strength until intelligence supports them.

Operational zoom is now a literal battalion handoff rather than a random encounter generator. Each operational battalion owns a persistent platoon-scale OOB: 1/4 has nine rifle platoons, two weapons platoons, and its attached combat engineers; 2/4 has nine rifle platoons, two weapons platoons, and its attached scout platoon. PLA amphibious combined-arms and reconnaissance battalions have distinct twelve- and eleven-platoon organizations. **Resolve Contact** places every platoon from the selected friendly battalion and the exact contacted enemy battalion on terrain extracted from that island hex, with no force-package picker. Every tactical platoon keeps its operational ID and prior strength, and its exact remaining strength is written back after battle; aggregate battalion strength is calculated from those records. Tactical-save schema 4 preserves both parent battalion IDs across save/restore. The randomized company configurator remains confined to explicitly standalone Quick Battles.

Opening a friendly battalion’s order menu and choosing Move reveals the area reachable with its remaining action points; hovering a legal destination previews the deterministic least-cost path, and left-clicking confirms the move. Terrain-weighted movement spends AP, the counter visibly enters a spent state at zero AP, and End Turn restores both friendly battalions’ allowances before resolving hidden PLA movement and refreshing the maneuver-level intelligence picture.

Package 1 expands tactical command from a single demonstration platoon to a reinforced company. A fresh standalone battle begins with force organization: three rifle platoons form the fixed core and the player chooses a Weapons, Reconnaissance, or Combat Engineer platoon as the attachment. The Weapons Platoon carries eight ammunition units, the Reconnaissance Platoon receives ten AP and cheaper Recon tasking, and the Combat Engineer Platoon receives nine AP ahead of its later breaching/fieldworks rules. All four counters are platoon-scale formations, deploy independently within a three-hex friendly zone, retain their own position, AP, readiness, cohesion, ammunition, and selection state, and persist through save/restore. Left- or right-click selects a platoon, **N** cycles the company, and **Tab** selects the next actionable platoon; a live company list provides the same controls without hunting across the map. The active platoon continues to use the proven Move, Inspect LOS, Direct Fire, Rally, Recon, and LOS-overlay orders.

Package 2 turns that company into a fire-and-maneuver force. Every platoon can cycle between **Quick**, **Tactical**, and **Bounding** movement: Quick doubles route reach but halves charged terrain cost and forfeits reaction reserve; Tactical is the baseline; Bounding trades reach for security and replenishes a reaction point. Each platoon also carries a two-point reaction reserve, a six-sector facing, and **Weapons Hold**, **Return Fire**, or **Weapons Free** policy. Those choices govern both PLA interruptions during USMC movement and USMC interruptions during observed PLA movement, with forward, flank, and rear-sector modifiers. The expanded order menu adds free facing changes, two-ammunition area suppression against a terrain hex, two-turn organic smoke within 500 m that obscures LOS, and adjacent close assault resolved against defender suppression and cover. All state and new event histories persist in battlefield schema 14, and `--fire-maneuver-regression` proves the linked rules.

Every button in the operational and tactical right-click order menus now provides a mouse-over field explanation. The tooltip describes the order's purpose, important costs or prerequisites, and the tactical reason to use it; disabled orders retain their explanation so the player can understand why that capability matters before it becomes available. Tooltips work identically with the full HUD shown or hidden.

Package 3 separates lasting combat power from temporary cohesion. Every platoon now carries persistent strength and supply alongside suppression, ammunition, AP, and readiness. Hasty, Standard, Deliberate, and Rapid attack profiles trade action points and ammunition for accuracy, suppression, and strength damage. Platoons can construct two levels of fighting positions, reorganize by spending time and supply, or resupply beside the Weapons Platoon; its original immediate-fire mechanic has since been superseded by Package 7's planned fires. A Reserve support card now places a fresh fifth rifle platoon on the battlefield instead of merely adding AP. Strength, positions, logistics, fire missions, and their chronological events use battlefield schema 15 and are covered by `--combat-power-regression`.

Package 4 adds the standalone longevity layer. The pre-battle company screen is now a Quick Battle configurator for mission, enemy quality, weather, light level, turn limit, attachment, and an optional interactive tutorial. Rain, storms, dawn/dusk, and night alter detection range and fire effectiveness; Veteran and Elite settings strengthen the opposition. Every standalone battle receives secondary objectives, records a richer AAR with remaining combat power, ammunition expenditure, fire missions, highlights, and objective completion, and updates persistent named-platoon career records with battles, victories, experience, and carried strength. The entire quick-battle definition is serializable and can be loaded from JSON with `--quick-battle=<path>`, providing the data-driven scenario foundation; `--longevity-regression` covers conditions, difficulty, objectives, tutorial content, and schema-16 serialization.

Package 5 connects observation, covered terrain, reaction fire, and cohesion into a concealment-and-morale loop. A stationary platoon in Medium or Heavy cover can spend two AP to Hide and prepare an ambush; concealment degrades enemy contact quality until movement, firing, or an adjacent Search/Clear order reveals it. The first direct or reaction shot from a prepared ambush gains accuracy and suppression bonuses, and every reveal, search, ambush, controlled withdrawal, forced fallback, rout, or surrender is recorded in the event log and AAR. Withdraw lets a platoon spend its activation displacing one hex farther from the nearest enemy while recovering cohesion. A formation that becomes Reduced attempts an immediate one-hex fallback toward safer ground; if surrounded beside the enemy it surrenders, otherwise it routs in place. The PLA can hide, search, and withdraw under the same legal-order system. Package 5 state and history use battlefield schema 17, and `--concealment-morale-regression` covers concealment, contact degradation, ambush effects, Search/Clear, fallback selection, and serialization.

Package 6 adds platoon-scale obstacles and battlefield engineering. Offensive battles against a PLA force receive a deterministic six-hex defensive layout of minefields, wire belts, and roadblocks around the objective. Hidden obstacles do not leak into route previews; first contact identifies the belt, halts movement, and applies posture-sensitive mine or wire effects. The Combat Engineer Platoon carries five engineer-supply points and can Detect/Mark adjacent ground, attempt a fast 70%-reliable Hasty Breach, or spend more time and stores on a guaranteed Deliberate Breach. Successful work leaves a persistent green safe lane used by route planning and the PLA AI. Defend missions instead allow three USMC obstacles to be placed near the objective before battle, while engineer-led Attack missions can earn a breach-lane secondary objective. Fighting positions now protect their chosen front fully, their flanks partially, and their rear not at all; engineers receive a close-assault bonus against covered or entrenched strongpoints. All obstacle intelligence, stores, lanes, placements, facing, and chronological events persist in battlefield schema 18, appear in the event log and AAR, and are covered by `--obstacles-regression`; all 29 regression suites pass together.

Package 7 replaces instant artillery with planned, readable fire support. A platoon spends 3 AP to request HE, Smoke, or Illumination; the requested hex receives a friendly aim-point marker and the mission arrives after the enemy phase, with scatter determined by contact quality, observer skill, and prior adjustment. Adjust Fire spends 1 AP to improve repeat missions on the same point. Supporting fires carry four rounds and a two-turn reload interval, while danger-close missions can affect friendly platoons inside the blast area. The Weapons Platoon separately carries four organic mortar rounds, must spend 2 AP emplacing before firing, loses that setup when it moves, and can deliver delayed HE or smoke from 2–6 hexes. Illumination improves observation in a three-hex area for two turns. PLA formations can evade visible aim points and use smoke or area suppression before maneuver. Planned missions, adjustment, ammunition, illumination, mortar state, and chronological events persist in battlefield schema 19 and are covered by `--planned-fires-regression`.

Any selected land hex can now open a deterministic 25×19 local battlefield. Its 475 hexes use 250 m geographic spacing and retain a versioned battlefield ID, parent operational hex, center/origin coordinates, bounds, terrain, and elevation samples. A short command-table transition leads to a local briefing card, amplified local relief, continuous coastal classification and shoreline accents, local hex inspection, bounded camera navigation, and a Return to Island control that restores the exact prior overview camera.

Each USMC platoon deploys with eight tactical AP. Local movement combines destination terrain and elevation-change costs, rejects water, slopes over 90 m per edge, friendly or enemy occupied cells, out-of-bounds destinations, and unaffordable routes, and persists completed, rejected, and cancelled orders by acting platoon. A continuous teal-to-gold route ribbon, destination ghost, reachable-area wash, live AP label, invalid red state, and terrain-following counter animation keep every order legible.

The tactical order menu also provides an inspect-only line-of-sight tool with a 12-hex (3 km) limit. It traces a deterministic hex line between observer and target, compares the interpolated sightline against measured elevation and rough-terrain obstruction, and reports clear, obscured, blocked, out-of-range, and map-edge results without committing an attack. Teal, amber, and red line segments and intervening-hex washes make the visibility transition readable directly on the terrain, while the inspection card lists the range and decisive modifiers.

Pass 6 adds an authoritative tactical observation picture. Every local hex receives terrain- and LOS-aware fog shading, while two opposing formations are represented according to what the USMC observer actually knows: hidden, uncertain contact, identified unit, or currently observed unit. Lost tracks persist for one turn at their last-known position before expiring. Contact records retain observer, range, LOS, turn, and stale-state data through JSON save/reload, and only a non-stale currently observed target passes the combat-information gate reserved for direct fire in Pass 7.

Pass 7 activates that combat-information gate with a deterministic M27 small-arms action. Direct fire costs 2 AP and one of six abstract ammunition units. A pre-fire panel exposes the hit chance, expected effect, range, terrain, and LOS modifiers before confirmation; seeded resolution produces a miss, suppression, or hit event. The fire line, target reticle, restrained muzzle/impact cue, counter response, ammunition display, and persistent versioned event record all present the same authoritative result.

Tactical units now use compact procedural formation models inspired by Sea of Uncertainty's counter language instead of flat blocks. The USMC rifle platoon and identified PLA formations combine three maneuver elements, a command node, affiliation-specific recognition stripe, designation plate, soft shadow, and selection halo. Uncertain enemy information remains a diamond contact rather than revealing formation geometry. The whole-island operational counter is intentionally unchanged in this first tactical-only art pass.

Pass 8 gives direct fire lasting consequences. Every Suppressed or Hit outcome now adds cumulative suppression points to the target, moving it through Ready, Suppressed, Disrupted, and Reduced cohesion states with their own movement and fire restrictions: Disrupted units cannot return fire, and a Reduced unit cannot move or fire at all until it recovers. A Rally order spends one AP to shake off a fixed amount of suppression, cohesion also decays passively at the start of every turn, and every application is a persistent, serializable event rather than a one-off die roll. Suppressed formations carry a pulsing status badge and a desaturated body color on the tactical counter and on any identified or observed enemy marker, the command card reports the unit's own status and point total once it leaves Ready, the direct-fire target tooltip previews the enemy's current cohesion, and the tactical intelligence panel appends a known enemy's status next to its contact state.

Every player action now has a rudimentary audio cue. Short tones are synthesized at runtime (no imported sound assets) for selecting a hex, opening an order menu, entering a planning mode, confirming or rejecting a move, each direct-fire outcome, rallying, ending a turn, transitioning to or from the tactical map, and a contact being gained or lost.

Pass 9 lets the enemy shoot back while the platoon is moving. Every eligible PLA formation now carries its own small-arms weapon, and each move checks the hexes the platoon actually steps into for an enemy that is still able to fire and has an unblocked, in-range line of sight; the nearest eligible reactor (ties broken by unit ID) takes exactly one snap-shot reaction per move, resolved with the same deterministic small-arms model as deliberate fire but at a shorter range and an accuracy penalty. A miss lets the platoon complete its route; a suppressing or damaging hit halts it at the exposed hex and applies the same cumulative suppression as Pass 8. The interruption pauses the route animation, pans and tightens the camera on the firer, raises an on-screen reaction-fire banner, flashes the reactor's marker and the platoon's own counter, and logs a persistent, replayable reaction event alongside the movement record it cut short.

Pass 10 closes the turn loop with a deterministic, objective-aware PLA turn. Ending the tactical turn hands control to every opposing formation in a fixed order; each one plans through the same legal-command interface the player uses, prioritizing recovery when disrupted, deliberate fire when a target is currently observed, objective-seeking movement along the cheapest legal route when neither applies, and an inspect-only observation update otherwise, with every candidate order re-validated against current board state before it executes. Visible enemy actions pan and tighten the camera on the acting unit and animate at normal speed; actions the platoon cannot currently observe resolve instantly behind a "hidden activity" summary, and a cinematic/fast toggle lets a player skip ahead once the pattern is familiar. A turn banner tracks phase and side, every AI action is logged as a persistent, replayable event alongside the existing movement, fire, suppression, and reaction records, and a fixed-seed planner replay, illegal-order rejection, and a hard planning-time budget are all covered by headless regression.

Pass 11 gives every 250 m tactical map real terrain texture. Each land hex now deterministically rolls a Light, Medium, or Heavy cover density — weighted by its Open/Rough/Highland terrain type, and reproducible from the battlefield's own ID so the same operational hex always regenerates the same layout — plus a chance of joining a small or medium built-up cluster of two to seven hexes wherever it falls within three hexes of shore. Cover is entirely independent of movement cost: it instead adds obstruction height and obscures line of sight, and meaningfully reduces hit and suppression chance for a defender in both deliberate and reaction fire, up to a 32-point hit-chance penalty for Heavy cover. Cover-bearing hexes carry a subtle color tint plus procedural vegetation clumps or, for built-up cells, small boxy structures with a contrasting roofline, and the LOS/fire inspection panels report the target's cover alongside every other modifier.

Pass 12 lets a tactical battle actually end. Every local map now deterministically assigns an Attack or Defend posture and a single objective hex — Attack places it as far from the platoon's own landing zone as the terrain allows without sitting next to a PLA start position, Defend simply asks the platoon to hold the ground it lands on — plus a short turn limit. A gold ring marks the objective and turns green the instant the platoon physically occupies it, a teal ring marks the platoon's own deployment zone when the two differ, and the command card reports the battle's turn count against its limit alongside a seize/hold status line. The battle concludes the moment the platoon or every observed PLA formation is fought to a standstill, or the turn limit expires, whichever comes first: a full-screen after-action screen reports victory, defeat, a mutual-destruction draw, or an inconclusive stalemate, alongside turns taken, casualties on both sides, and a chronological log merged from every movement, fire, suppression, reaction, and enemy-action record of the battle.

Attack and Defend objective victory now requires **three consecutive completed turns of control**, replacing the original check for who happened to occupy the hex when the turn limit expired. Progress is credited only when the PLA phase hands control back to USMC, so moving onto the objective starts the defense rather than instantly winning; surviving three enemy responses advances the visible counter through 1/3, 2/3, and 3/3 to secure victory. Losing the hex resets progress to zero. If the original scenario deadline arrives while an Attack or Defend objective remains unresolved, the battle enters a single three-turn overtime window so an active hold still has enough time to mature; the extended deadline remains finite and produces a stalemate if neither side resolves the objective. The command card exposes the live hold count and overtime state, while each advance, reset, and extension is persisted in the objective state and chronological event log. Raid, Reconnaissance-in-Force, Withdrawal, and combat-ineffectiveness outcomes retain their distinct rules.

Pass 13 adds a Recon order without adding a new unit — ISR drones stay off the table for now. The platoon can task a focused sensor sweep on any hex up to 12 hexes away for 3 AP, and unlike every other spotting tool this one needs no ground line of sight, representing an indirect or overhead request rather than the platoon's own eyes. A successful tasking grants a one-tier detection bonus — Hidden to Contact, Contact to Identified, Identified to Observed — on that exact hex for two turns, marked by a violet ring distinct from the objective and deployment-zone rings; because the bonus is tied to the hex rather than the formation on it, a PLA element that moves off the tasked ground stops benefiting from it, a deliberate limit rather than a gap. The order, its AP cost, its expiry, and the resulting contact-state change are all persistent, replayable events alongside the rest of the battle log.

Pass 14 completes Phase I's paired-implementation passes with a stub file-based round trip to Sea of Uncertainty — the theater location, forces, posture, seed, and objective a versioned `BattleRequest` can carry, not the full production contract (equipment, ammunition, experience, and the rest) that's a later milestone. Launching with `--battle-request=<path>` reads and validates the file, shows a brief campaign-briefing overlay with the imported request/campaign ID, theater hex, posture, and objective, then imports the platoon's and both PLA formations' IDs and display names wherever the request supplies them (the command card marks an imported platoon "IMPORTED"; the rest stay hidden behind fog of war like any other contact). A request's seed reseeds the whole battle — cover, posture, and objective placement together — by folding into the same battlefield ID every other deterministic system already hashes, so the identical request always reproduces the identical battle. The moment the battle concludes, its `BattleResult` — outcome, objective control, casualties, turns taken, and the same merged event log as the after-action screen — is written atomically (temp file, then rename) to the request's output path exactly once, and the after-action screen's dismiss button becomes "Return to Campaign" with the exported path shown. An invalid, malformed, or version-mismatched request is rejected with a readable on-screen error and a "Continue Standalone" fallback rather than a crash.

With Phase I's paired-implementation passes complete, save and restore closes out the first item on the polish and release gate. A **Save** button on the tactical command card writes the entire live battle — terrain, event history, objective/victory state, the platoon and both PLA formations with their weapons, the current contact picture, the turn counter, and (when the battle was launched from a `BattleRequest`) that request itself — to a single save slot, atomically, the same way a `BattleResult` is written. The operational map's command card shows a **Continue Saved Battle** button whenever a save exists; choosing it rebuilds the exact tactical scene, including view elements a fresh battle would otherwise generate anew (recon rings, contact markers, occupancy), then resumes the observation and event-sequence bookkeeping precisely where the save left off rather than re-deriving anything. Saving is disabled once a battle has concluded, and a corrupted or out-of-bounds save file is rejected with the same readable on-screen error used for a bad `BattleRequest`.

An audit pass closes out the polish and release gate's feedback item. A rejected Direct Fire or Recon click — previously a silent no-op if the target was invalid, out of range, or unaffordable — now re-affirms the same rejection reason already shown on hover, marks the target hex invalid, and plays a cue, exactly like a rejected Move order always has. Gaining or losing the objective hex is now a persistent, replayable `OBJECTIVE` event with its own cue, not just a ring color change nobody might notice. The battle's own outcome now appears as the final line of its own chronological event log, not only on the separate after-action screen. Log lines read by unit name rather than raw ID, and a rejected move's specific reason is folded into its log line instead of just the terse outcome. Save, load, and every battle-request/save-load error now play a cue like every other order, closing the last silent gaps.

Every explicitly standalone Quick Battle varies. Its scenario seed controls cover, posture, objective placement, turn limit, and the generated PLANMC force. Operational zoom battles do not use that generator: their deterministic seed comes from the island scenario, turn, hex, and participating battalion IDs, and their roster comes from the persistent campaign OOB. Leaving mid-battle and coming back reproduces the exact same engagement. A `--scenario-seed=<N>` flag can still pin a standalone setup for testing or sharing.

Formation command is authoritative throughout the battle rather than only presentational. Friendly LOS combines into one fog-of-war picture; contacts identify the observing platoon; any friendly platoon can seize and hold the objective; defeat requires the whole friendly force to become combat ineffective; and the PLA evaluates every friendly platoon while maneuvering toward the objective. Tactical-save schema 4 stores the complete roster, weapons, selection state, and operational parent binding. Schema-2 and schema-3 saves remain loadable, and `BattleRequest` battles deliberately retain their original one-USMC/two-PLA contract roster until that external contract is versioned.

Standalone play now has real mission variety and a lightweight campaign layer above it. Beyond Attack and Defend, a fresh scenario can roll **Raid** (win by breaking at least half the opposing roster, not by holding ground — a later PLA recovery can't retroactively undo an achieved raid), **Reconnaissance-in-Force** (win by identifying the entire opposing roster at least once, rewarding Recon tasking and LOS play over direct combat), or **Withdrawal** (win by platoon survival to the turn limit, regardless of who holds the objective hex) — drawn straight from the cross-boundary mission catalog already worked out in design planning. A `--mission=attack|defend|raid|recon|withdrawal` flag can force one for testing; `BattleRequest`-driven battles are entirely unaffected, always a plain Attack/Defend mirror of their posture. Sitting above individual scenarios, a persistent **Battalion status** — file-backed, surviving an app restart until deliberately reset — tracks strength from 0-100% across every generated standalone battle: a clean win nudges it up, a costly win, stalemate, draw, or defeat drags it down (never below a 20% floor), and a Battalion under 60% strength fields its next platoon two action points light until it recovers. A toast announces the mission type and Battalion status at the start of every fresh scenario, both maps show the current strength, the after-action screen reports the change, and a **Reset Campaign** button on the operational map starts the campaign over.

A persistent **Settings** panel, now covering a complete accessibility pass, is reachable from a bottom-left button on both maps. It covers UI scale (Auto, which reproduces the original screen-height-derived sizing exactly, plus fixed Small/Normal/Large/Extra-Large tiers) and remappable Cancel and Reset Camera keys, the two single-bound essential controls where a bad default could genuinely strand a player (camera pan already has a redundant WASD-and-Arrow-keys binding, so it isn't remapped). Rebinding captures the next key you press via `Event.current` inside `OnGUI` and rejects binding both actions to the same key with visible feedback rather than allowing ambiguity. Settings persist the same way the battle save and Battalion status do — atomically, to a file under `Application.persistentDataPath` — with a Reset to Defaults button and a `--settings-path=<path>` override for scripted runs.

A second pass on the same panel adds **Graphics Preset** (Low/Medium/High, scaling tactical-map cover/built-up prop density and terrain contour shading — never gameplay values), **Color-Safe Palette** (swaps the unit-status, spotted-tier, and LOS-segment colors from a single warm red/orange/yellow ramp, which reads as pure brightness under red-green color-vision deficiency, to a blue/orange/magenta spread with a distinct hue per severity tier), **Reduced Motion** (drops the movement step's vertical bob and snaps the reaction-fire camera pan instantly instead of easing it), and **Animation Speed** (Normal/Fast/Skip, scaling movement steps, fire-line animation, and reaction-fire camera pans; also feeds the existing per-session "ENEMY SPEED" toggle's baseline pacing rather than replacing it). All four persist to the same settings file as UI scale and key remaps.

The tactical HUD picked up four situational-awareness additions. A **LOG** button next to Settings/Support opens a live, collapsible event-log panel reading the same chronological log the after-action screen builds from the battlefield's typed event lists — previously that log was only visible once a battle had already ended. A **FOCUS OBJECTIVE** button in the Tactical Intelligence panel snaps the camera straight to the objective hex. The AMMO badge on the command card now colors orange when a weapon is running low and red when empty, and a **REACTION FIRE • READY/UNAVAILABLE** badge sits next to it, reflecting the exact eligibility condition `TacticalReactionFire` itself checks rather than a separate approximation. A sixth tactical-menu button, **LOS OVERLAY**, tints every hex in range Clear/Obscured/Blocked from the selected unit's current position without needing to pick a target first; because it drives the same per-cell tint as the pre-existing single-target INSPECT LOS tool, the two are kept mutually exclusive — starting either one cancels the other — and the overlay clears itself whenever the unit moves or a new battle is entered so it can never show a stale picture.

Pressing **H** on either map collapses the complete left/right information HUD into two narrow edge tabs for an unobstructed battlefield view. Pressing **H** again—or clicking either tab—restores those panels. A unit's right-click order menu is independent: opening it never forces the HUD visible, and it remains the only full menu shown while the HUD is hidden. Collapsing still closes optional Settings, Support, Event Log, and OOB panels; modal briefings, deployment, results, and other required decision screens stay visible. H is reserved and cannot be assigned to Cancel or Reset Camera.

Every counter, cover clump, and building on the tactical map now has real form instead of a flat silhouette. A close-zoom capture showed the problem plainly: every primitive-built piece — the platoon/formation counters, vegetation clumps, buildings — used the same completely unlit overlay shader as HUD rings and lines, so spheres and cubes rendered as flat blobs with no light or shadow at all. A new **MapSolid** shader adds a cheap fixed-direction lambert term for exactly these solid pieces (counters, cover props) while leaving rings, LOS lines, fire lines, and other flat overlay effects on the original unlit shader, where a flat "glow" look is actually correct. Separately, the ground itself picked up subtle procedural grain in `MapTerrain.shader` — world-position hash noise, no texture asset, matching this codebase's flat-primitive/no-imported-assets style — plus a softened hex side-wall shading floor so raised-hex edges read as a rim rather than a near-black outline up close. Confirmed with side-by-side captures at both close tactical zoom and the whole-island overview scale, where the grain stays imperceptible.

Counters now have a second, switchable presentation skin. A new **Counter Skin** setting (Illustrated / NATO Symbol) lets a player swap the hand-built Broken Front-style counter for a `NatoSymbolView` alternate — a doctrinal blue-friendly/red-hostile frame with an infantry-cross or support-dot icon, independent of this game's own illustrated green-USMC/red-PLA palette. It switches live for the player's platoon and every PLA contact marker, in sync down to detection tier and combat-status coloring: the player's counter self-updates every frame from its bound `TacticalUnitState` (so it needed no wiring into the ~20 scattered `Present()` call sites the illustrated skin uses), while PLA contacts are driven explicitly from the same `ContactMarkerView.Present()` that already colors the illustrated model. PLA rifle squads and support teams are now visually distinct in both skins too — a protruding barrel prop on the illustrated model, a solid dot instead of a cross on the symbol skin — derived from the same role string in both places. Not full MIL-STD-2525 fidelity (no size/echelon glyph or mobility modifiers), and documented as such.

The Symbol skin now billboards instead of lying flat on the hex. Real NATO/MIL-STD symbology is read face-on, like a paper counter — the flat-on-the-ground version from the first pass looked like a foreshortened decal from the tactical camera's angle. Since that camera's viewing angle never orbits (only pans and zooms — see `ApplyCamera`), a single fixed tilt, `Quaternion.FromToRotation(Vector3.up, cameraOffsetDirection)`, is enough to carry the whole frame/icon/label/status-badge group to face the camera everywhere on the board; no per-frame billboard math needed. A small separate ground-anchor dot stays flat on the hex underneath so it's still obvious which hex the unit occupies. Confirmed by capture: the frame now reads as an undistorted square instead of a foreshortened parallelogram.

Counters now have a third skin, **Miniature**, built from real sculpted USMC/PLANMC figures instead of procedural primitives — this codebase's first use of imported 3D assets. The USMC Rifleman, PLANMC Rifleman, and PLANMC Mortar Team OBJ models live under `Assets/Resources/Models/OneStar`, reused from the sibling `down_range_campaign` project's "OneStar" Down Range miniature pack (© Nicholas Royer, **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** — NonCommercial and ShareAlike; full terms and attribution in `NOTICE.md` alongside the models). They're lit by the scene's existing directional lights via Unity's Standard shader, the one piece in this game that isn't flat/unlit, with glossiness and metallic zeroed out for a matte, painted-miniature look. `MiniatureCounterView` plugs into the same skin-switching machinery the NATO Symbol skin already uses — a third sibling per counter, toggled the same way, with the player's counter self-driving from its bound `TacticalUnitState` and PLA contacts driven explicitly by `ContactMarkerView`. Model scale and brightness were both tuned empirically against captures (the first attempt read too small and too dark); a follow-up pass then split the rifle role into a small cluster of four of that same figure (scattered and yaw-jittered, echoing `TacticalFormationView`'s own three-element wedge) rather than one dominant soldier, so it reads as a squad rather than a single giant clone — the support-role Mortar Team keeps its single instance, since that sculpt is already a multi-figure crew. A further pass spread the cluster noticeably wider for a "small tabletop vignette" feel and added a vehicle/drone prop to each side's scene as set dressing: a USMC Black Hornet drone hovers over the USMC scene (the source pack has no USMC ground vehicle, only this drone and some faction-unclear generic quadcopters), and a PLANMC ZBL-09 wheeled IFV sits beside the PLA support-team crew specifically, not the rifle squad. The ZBL-09's texture looks like a checkerboard at a glance; it's actually a legitimate blue digital naval camouflage pattern, plausible for a PLA *Navy* Marine Corps vehicle. **If Always Faithful ever moves toward a commercial release, these specific model files need to be replaced or separately licensed first** — see `NOTICE.md` for the exact constraint.

USMC specialist attachments now have their own Down Range miniature compositions. The Weapons Platoon uses the source scenario's M249 and MAAWS gunners instead of generic riflemen. The Combat Engineer Platoon uses an EW Operator, an officer, and two riflemen as a recognizable technical/command team; the EW sculpt uses the source project's triplanar USMC field-camouflage treatment because that converted print has no authored UV texture. These are visual identities only—the platoons' game rules remain unchanged.

The miniature vignette's ZBL-09 is subsequently extended from support teams to every identified PLANMC rifle or support counter. This keeps randomly generated all-rifle rosters visually mechanized; the vehicle remains representative set dressing on the formation counter, not an independently targetable or simulated unit.

Both maps' cameras now rotate. **Q/E** orbits yaw and **Page Up/Down** tilts pitch (originally clamped 15°-85°), added alongside the existing WASD/middle-drag pan and scroll-wheel zoom — deliberately without roll, so hexes, labels, and counters never tip sideways. Both maps now share one spherical direction formula instead of each having a different fixed or zoom-curved camera offset; operational mode's old "auto-tilt steeper as you zoom out" behavior is gone, replaced by the same manual pitch control tactical mode now has too. Pan is yaw-relative, so WASD/drag still feels like screen-relative up/down/left/right once the camera has been rotated. Rotating surfaced a real dependency: the NATO Symbol skin's billboard tilt used to be a one-time-computed constant that assumed the camera's angle never changed. It's now recomputed every frame against the camera's live position, and, while in there, was also fixed to use world rotation rather than local — the player's own counter root already gets rotated to face its last movement direction, and the card needs to face the camera regardless of that. Confirmed by capture at a rotated angle: the map genuinely renders from the new viewpoint, and the NATO frame still reads as an undistorted square.

A subsequent camera-control pass opens that orbit for close terrain-level inspection. Plain **middle-drag** now provides direct two-axis orbit with unrestricted 360° yaw, while **Shift+middle-drag** pans and WASD/arrow panning remains available. Tactical panning is no longer confined to the original focus rectangle, tactical zoom spans 4-80 world units, operational zoom spans 8-320, and pitch reaches 2°-88°. A terrain-aware safety floor samples the nearest rendered hex under the camera (or the command table outside the board), lifting the eye just enough to prevent it passing below raised relief while still allowing a near-ground view. The wheel remains zoom, and Reset Camera remains the immediate route back to standard framing.

Standalone play now has an order of battle: a persistent **Battalion support hand**, drawn from an abstract resource pool of three card types — **ISR Sweep**, **Fire Support**, and **Reserve Platoon** — one card at a time, after every concluded engagement, up to four cards on hand. Whenever a fresh scenario begins with at least one card in hand, a **Commit Support?** modal offers the player the choice of playing exactly one card for a whole-battle effect before the usual mission briefing plays, or skipping: ISR elevates enemy detection for the whole battle, Fire Support applies a one-time prep-bombardment suppression to any enemy near the objective, and Reserve Platoon grants the platoon bonus starting action points. A read-only **Support** panel on either map shows the current hand at any time. `BattleRequest`-driven battles never draw, show, or spend a card. Package 7's on-call planned-fire pool is deliberately separate from these pre-battle cards.

ISR and reconnaissance are no longer one-sided. Every support card play now appends a readable `SUPPORT •` line to the same chronological event log every other order already uses, and the ISR card's whole-battle effect shows a persistent "ISR ACTIVE" tag next to the Battalion strength readout instead of vanishing the moment it's played. On the PLA side, a formation that loses contact with the platoon within the last turn can now task its own active sensor sweep — a **Recon** order mirroring the platoon's own, spending the same 3 AP and 2-turn duration on the platoon's last-known hex, marked with a distinct red ring so it's never mistaken for the platoon's own violet one. The PLA's own contact picture now persists turn to turn the same way the player's fog-of-war already does, and the tactical intelligence panel gains an "ENEMY TRACKING YOU" readout — amber, orange, or red by how well the enemy currently has you fixed — whenever any PLA formation holds a live, non-stale contact. None of this is gated to standalone play; it applies identically to a `BattleRequest`-driven battle, since it's core tactical resolution rather than campaign meta-state.

A local map now respects the operational picture exactly. **OPEN LOCAL MAP** carries the selected friendly battalion onto the real extracted terrain but includes no enemy when no battalion contact is present. **RESOLVE CONTACT** carries the exact contacted enemy battalion and all of its surviving platoons—never a substitute hash-generated roster. A dedicated `--no-contact-regression` check covers the empty-map distinction, while `--operational-scenario-regression` covers full OOB handoff and one-for-one loss rollup.

Terrain dressing at close tactical zoom went a step further, following straight on from the flat-cylinder-clump/color-only-ground gap the earlier lighting pass had left open. Natural cover now builds actual tree and bush shapes in `TacticalCoverView` instead of one flat cylinder per clump — Light cover reads as a low bush, Medium/Heavy cover as a trunk with a jittered two-or-three-lobe canopy cluster, so a hex's foliage silhouette finally looks like foliage. Rough and Highland ground gets scattered boulder clutter keyed off the terrain class itself rather than the vegetation-cover roll, so bare rocky ground can still read as rocky even where nothing grew there. A later refinement replaces the original cut-stone cubes with flattened, irregular rounded boulders and limits them to a deterministic 34% of Rough hexes and 62% of Highland hexes at the default graphics preset (with small Low/High density adjustments), preventing the landscape from becoming uniformly cluttered. A small, purely cosmetic pond-and-reeds prop now appears on a low-probability slice of ordinary land hexes — never Highland, built-up, or real open water — giving the ground a little variety without touching movement, LOS, or any saved battlefield data; it's decoration in exactly the same sense as the existing cover clumps and buildings. Real open-water hexes finally move: a new `MapWater` shader replaces the static grain they used to share with land with a time-scrolled two-layer ripple plus sparse bright glints, its strength following the Graphics Preset the same way land's elevation-contour shading already does (off at Low). All four were confirmed by capture at both close and whole-board zoom, including the coastline water tile rendering its expected color with no shader errors.

The next scene-composition pass gives that dressing a coherent place in the map instead of letting it float above it. Tactical trees, rocks, ponds, and buildings now use a dedicated depth-tested `MapProp` shader, so nearby relief correctly occludes distant scenery, and each prop inherits its parent hex's fog-of-war amount instead of glowing at full saturation in unknown ground. Their silhouettes are scaled up enough to read at the normal tactical framing. Land and water receive a restrained inset hex bevel, broad tonal variation, and graphics-preset-scaled distance atmosphere; water's blocky scrolling noise is replaced with intersecting directional wavelets and sparse glints. The operational map keeps its existing clean cartographic treatment because the new edge and atmospheric controls are enabled only on tactical materials.

## Sea of Uncertainty developer flow

Launch a battle from a file instead of the operational map with `AlwaysFaithful.exe --battle-request=<path-to-request.json>`. The `BattleRequest`/`BattleResult` JSON shapes are `AlwaysFaithful.Core.BattleRequest`/`BattleResult` (`Assets/Scripts/Core/TacticalBattleContract.cs`) — `TheaterHex`, `Seed`, optional posture/objective/turn-limit overrides, and an optional `Forces` list of `{ Role, UnitId, DisplayName }` entries (`Role` one of `usmc-rifle-platoon`, `pla-rifle-squad`, `pla-support-team`). The result is written once, atomically, to the request's `OutputPath` the moment the battle concludes. This is the Phase I stub of the broader Sea of Uncertainty integration boundary — theater, forces, posture, seed, and objective only, not the full production contract.

The in-progress-battle save file defaults to `Application.persistentDataPath/always-faithful-battle-save.json`; override it with `--save-path=<path>` (useful for pointing a regression or a scripted run at a scratch file instead of the real save slot). Its `AlwaysFaithful.Core.TacticalBattleSaveState` shape (`Assets/Scripts/Core/TacticalBattleSave.cs`) wraps the full `TacticalBattlefieldState` plus everything else a live session needs to resume: turn state, both sides' units and weapons, the contact picture, the running event sequence number, and the active `BattleRequest` if the saved battle was launched from one.

The persistent Battalion status file defaults to `Application.persistentDataPath/always-faithful-battalion-status.json`; override it with `--battalion-status-path=<path>` for the same reason. Its `AlwaysFaithful.Core.TacticalBattalionStatus` shape (`Assets/Scripts/Core/TacticalBattalion.cs`) is a strength value, a history of concluded engagements, and the support-card hand (`AlwaysFaithful.Core.TacticalSupportCard`, `Assets/Scripts/Core/TacticalSupportCards.cs`) — small enough to read or hand-edit directly for testing a specific strength band or a specific hand.

The operational scenario file defaults to `Application.persistentDataPath/always-faithful-operational-scenario.json`; override it with `--operational-scenario-path=<path>`. Its `AlwaysFaithful.Core.OperationalScenarioState` shape (`Assets/Scripts/Core/OperationalScenario.cs`) owns the operations order, objective, both orders of battle, true battalion positions and strength, the player’s incomplete enemy-contact picture, active operational recon areas, and operational turn number. Reset Campaign rebuilds this scenario and the standalone Battalion status together.

The settings file defaults to `Application.persistentDataPath/always-faithful-settings.json`; override it with `--settings-path=<path>` for the same reason. Its `AlwaysFaithful.Core.AlwaysFaithfulSettings` shape (`Assets/Scripts/Core/AlwaysFaithfulSettings.cs`) currently holds the UI scale tier and the two remappable key bindings.

## Geography strategy

The prototype carries a full-Taiwan crop from the same Natural Earth coastline and NOAA ETOPO 2022 source pipeline used by Sea of Uncertainty so the games share geographic provenance and a related command-map palette. A dedicated two-sided cartographic terrain shader renders the complete board consistently from strategic and tactical camera angles. Land elevation uses subtle 250 m contour bands, water uses measured shelf/slope/abyss depth bands, and true land-water edges receive a two-stage wet-shore/coast highlight.

The current ETOPO bake is two arc-minutes (roughly 3–4 km sample spacing) and the coastline is simplified for an operational map. The 250 m local grids presently resample and visually amplify these shared sources, which preserves geographic identity and shoreline continuity but does not invent tactical-detail accuracy. Higher-resolution DEM and vector data will later replace those samples for tactical cover and LOS.

Regenerate the checked-in Taiwan resources with `tools/build-taiwan-coastline.cjs` and `tools/build-taiwan-elevation.ps1`.
